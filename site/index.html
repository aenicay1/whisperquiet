The point is local.

WhisperFlow-style tools proved people want fast dictation everywhere. The annoying part is paying a monthly cloud bill for something a modern Mac can already run privately.

Cloud dictation

  • Audio has to leave your machine before text comes back.
  • Useful features get bundled into another subscription.
  • Network quality becomes part of your writing tool.

WhisperQuiet

  • Speech runs on-device after the first model download.
  • No account, usage meter, or transcription dashboard.
  • Text lands directly into whatever app is already active.

By default WhisperQuiet keeps each recording's audio (capped at 512 MB / 30 days) and transcripts on-device to improve accuracy over time. Nothing leaves your Mac, and you can turn either off in config.

Built for actual writing.

This is not a meeting bot or a recorder. It is a small menu-bar app for turning spoken thoughts into text wherever your cursor already is.

01 / private by default

What happens when you dictate.

The app keeps the workflow intentionally boring. Press a key, capture audio, transcribe locally, clean the text, type it into the active app.

Opening the preview.

This build is ad-hoc signed, not notarized. macOS will be dramatic the first time. That is expected for now.

  1. 01Download and unzip WhisperQuiet.zip, then drag WhisperQuiet into Applications.
  2. 02macOS 15 (Sequoia) or newer: open the app once, macOS will block it, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to the "WhisperQuiet was blocked" notice, click Open Anyway, and confirm (you may be asked to authenticate). Open the app again after that.
  3. 03macOS 13–14: right-click the app, choose Open, then choose Open again.
  4. 04Grant Microphone, Accessibility, and Input Monitoring. Quit and reopen once after granting permissions.
  5. 05Hold right Option, speak, release. If something breaks, that feedback is useful.
